# Roadmap

Work is tracked by version, numbered `major.minor.patch`. v1.4.0 is live in prod and v1.4.1 is open, filled as backlog items are promoted into it. The eleven tracks below cut across every version. A version ships only when its exit criterion is met, and each task carries an exit criterion, not a description. Only the open version carries checkboxes: a released version is condensed here to what it achieved, its reasoning kept in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) and its player-facing notes in `releases.ts`.

Statuses: `[ ]` not started, `[~]` in progress, `[x]` done.

## Tracks

1. `shared-protocol`: shared TS types, WS messages, piece and user schemas
2. `piece-generation`: Bezier silhouettes, snap geometry, piece types
3. `image-pipeline`: libvips processing, Deep Zoom tiles, per-piece AVIF, R2 upload
4. `frontend-shell`: landing, routing, entry flow, auth and options modals
5. `frontend-canvas`: PixiJS rendering, OpenSeadragon, drag/drop, LOD, culling
6. `backend-realtime`: WS server, drag/drop/snap logic, Redis state, Mongo logs, snapshots
7. `auth-and-accounts`: Auth.js (Google), guest players, pseudo onboarding, sessions, login anti-abuse
8. `infra-deploy`: Docker, Coolify, OVH, Cloudflare (Pages, R2, CDN, DNS)
9. `tooling-foundations`: monorepo workspaces, shared tsconfig, eslint, prettier
10. `qa-and-load`: load tests up to 1M simulated pieces and clients
11. `legal`: privacy policy, ToS, GDPR notes, license attributions

## Released

**Before v1.0.0.** A single player first completed a configurable-size puzzle locally with the full architecture running (WS server, Redis and Mongo under docker-compose). A closed alpha then put 5 to 20 invited people on a deployed 10 000-piece board, the performance work built as the real solution rather than a stopgap: drag coalescing, per-group dispatch queues, zoom-out LOD, per-IP rate limiting. The board then opened to the public with 1 000 000 unique pieces: the gigapixel image pipeline on R2, viewport-sharded broadcasts backed by a group index and partial-state resync, PixiJS LOD with viewport-driven texture streaming, Auth.js with Google, the admin ops page, the frontend on Cloudflare Pages, EN/FR/ES/DE throughout, the legal documents and load tests to 10 000 clients. Open access then removed the last barrier in front of the canvas: a first-time visitor places a piece with no OAuth redirect on a guest identity minted in-site, signing in with Google claims those contributions, and everyone is served by a single real-time path gated by an admission queue past a global connection cap.

**v1.0.0, Launch Readiness.** The board stopped being synthetic: three scripts (`fetch-tile-images.ts`, `ingest-unsplash-tiles.ts`, `build-mosaic.ts`) assemble a gigapixel BigTIFF photo mosaic from the NASA Blue Marble image and a CC0/Wikimedia/Unsplash tile library, with a provenance manifest and idempotent resume. Prod runs the 14.4 GP `earth-mosaic` output, a 1000x1000 grid at 120px per piece. Rendering and state were rebuilt around the locked region: locked pieces render from `DziRevealLayer` (the reference pyramid revealed through a server-baked silhouette mask), a locked piece lives on the piece hash rather than in a group, unlocked merges are capped at `MPP_CLUSTER_PIECE_CAP`, and locked-region state reaches clients as incremental server-composited per-cell tiles. A board whose stored `generationSeed` differs from the loaded one is relabeled at boot rather than served unplayable. The release also made the game findable and measurable: per-route SEO metadata, self-hosted Umami and its Postgres, a live connected-player count, independently skippable onboarding steps, the Unsplash and mosaic credits, and a clustered load-test run against prod with a clean visual and state-corruption verdict.

**v1.1.0.** Below 680px a phone gets the board instead of a HUD. The account menu gained a stepped tips strip, a scoring explainer and dated update notes. The standings went live behind a coalescing publisher (at most one publish per 2s window, top 100 deltas folded client-side, a personal standing for anyone ranked outside it), and an anchoring merge credits the whole set it locks so the standings add up to the full million. The canvas gained personal flags: up to 8 per browser, colored, each a camera jump and a drop target that lands a cluster on a searched clear patch. A player can also show the source photo faintly under the board, locked content casting a shadow onto that ghost. Signing in with Google promotes the guest document into a permanent account, reachable again from a second device through the onboarding pseudo step. A restart is announced on the wire before sockets drop, every client landing on a maintenance screen served entirely from Cloudflare Pages.

**v1.1.1.** A peer pointer with no update for 10s is hidden, and the next position places it outright rather than gliding it in from where it was last seen.

**v1.2.0.** Every HUD panel has its own switch in the Display section, a switched-off panel hidden where it stands, and `/play` lays out inside every viewport it opens in, verified by measured rects across 13 sizes from 320x568 to 1920x1080. An unread release marks the topbar gear and the Update notes row, a browser with nothing stored seeded as read. The landing's percentage floors to three decimals so the last 500 pieces no longer read 100%. The overview's expand opens the panel's own map enlarged, both drawn by one painter. Three server-side fixes closed the release: a lock now marks every world-grid cell the piece's box reaches into (cells baked before it keep their gap, no resweep planned), a flag drop is resolved by the server against its own complete indexes, and every socket close is logged with its code and cause on both ends.

**v1.3.0.** The game stopped speaking as a competition: no string in the four locales calls the standings a leaderboard, a ranking, a score or a point, and the overview is no longer called a minimap anywhere a player can read it (the wire keeps both names). A press on the enlarged overview aims the camera as the panel does, a click on the enlarged reference centers the board behind a crosshair toggle or Ctrl/Cmd, that reference keeps its border at every zoom, the board's own edge stops the camera where it becomes visible, and the zoom-out LOD band is grab-free. The landing polls a cacheable global `GET /live` through a Cloudflare Cache Rule instead of the per-IP route, and no longer renders a phase it has not confirmed. Two server rules closed the board's soft spots: a loose-loose merge is refused when more than `MPP_SNAP_COVER_MAX` other pieces cover the contact piece's centre, and `handleDrop` clamps the origin it persists into the play zone. Backups reach R2 for the first time (a bucket-scoped token refuses rclone's existence check, now skipped) and the sidecar joins Umami's and Coolify's networks. Both OpenSeadragon viewers request their tiles with CORS, and under 560px the topbar carries one thing per box.

**v1.4.0.** A player keeps a notebook of the spots they work in, opened from a topbar button left of the presence pill. A bookmark is taken by aiming at the board, a piece by default or a square of 4 to 24 pieces sized a piece a wheel notch under the cursor, badged with an extract of the reference picture rather than a capture, named, filed as it is written under up to five tags of the player's own words, starred, and reached again in a click at whatever scale they are reading the board at; the list is favorites first then by name and pages by 10, and a row's link hands the spot to someone else, landing them framed on the same point with the same bookmark as a prefilled draft they save under their own name. An entry records a place and no scale, draws nothing on the canvas or either overview, and is offered above the compact breakpoint alone. A hand also carries up to 10 pieces, Ctrl or Cmd clicking them onto the one already held, and a double-click lays them all down around that point, each on the free patch nearest it, the drag rate staying at one message a frame whatever the hand holds. The overview reads out the player's position in whole pieces from the centre of the frame, and the contributors panel frames a player ranked outside the leaders with the contributor immediately above and the one immediately below. A viewport arriving on a dense region fills outward from its centre, the hydrate queue partitioned on-screen ahead of margin ring with distance as the tie-break and texture loads admitted one per image-decode worker: on the local 1M board no load over 9s and some 85 a second sustained. A browser rendering the board in software raises a notice under the topbar after three unbroken seconds of frames over 66 ms and takes it back down once the frames come back, its dismissal permanent per browser. Both landing routes now carry the server clock at the read, so an older body is dropped whole and the locked count never falls back, and the landing's feed carries ten placements to fill the card it sits in. The account menu gained a Ko-fi row, and a backup pass writes one dated folder on R2, pruned to the newest three passes rather than copies of a kind.

## v1.4.1

**Exit criterion**: everything below is deployed and verified in prod.

- [x] `frontend-shell`: the enlarged reference names the board point under the cursor. Exit criterion: moving the mouse over the photo shows a readout that follows the cursor and gives the point it sits on in the coordinates the rest of the game uses, whole pieces from the centre of the frame, and no readout is shown off the photo. The window already turned a point of the photo into a camera jump, but until the click nothing said where that point was, so a player could aim at a spot without ever being able to name it or read it out to someone. The pyramid is the cropped source, so an image pixel maps 1:1 onto a world point and the reading is the conversion the pick already does, handed to `worldToBoard` and `formatBoardPoint`, the pair the overview's own row reads through. The viewer hands the point up on a native `pointermove` of its own rather than through OpenSeadragon, whose viewer raises no move event; its window-level capture handler swallows the move while a pan is under way, which is right, since the image travels with the cursor and the point under it does not move. The readout follows the cursor rather than sitting in a corner, since what it names is the pixel under the cursor, and it flips to the other side of the cursor at the right and bottom edges so it never hangs off the window; its width is held at the widest reading the frame can produce, as the zoom pillar's own row is, so it neither resizes under the player's hand nor makes the flip guess at a box it has not drawn yet. Mouse only: it is read by hovering, which a touch pointer cannot do. Verified on a local 1M board: the centre of the photo reads `(0, 0)`, its corners `(-498, -498)` and `(498, 498)` a pixel inside, its quarter point `(-250, -250)`, the readout stays inside the window against all four edges, and it goes out the moment the cursor leaves the photo for the viewer's own margin.

- [x] `frontend-shell`: a bookmark's name is optional, and written in place from its row. Exit criterion: an entry saved with the name field left empty is kept and reads under the first word it is filed under, under a stand-in while it wears none, and a row's name is corrected in the list itself rather than in a form of its own. A spot is taken in one click on the board, and the form then held that entry until the player produced a word for it, which is a word they often have not chosen yet; the tags they file it under are the same words they would have typed. The name is now empty by default and `bookmarkLabel` answers what everything shows: the player's own name, then the entry's first tag, then nothing, the panel putting its own localized "unnamed" in that last case rather than the notebook storing one locale's word. Tags are held alphabetically, so the borrowed word is the first indexed one: filing an unnamed entry under "Cat" names the row Cat, taking that word back off hands it to the next word, and `addTag`, `removeTag` and `renameBookmark` all re-sort like starring does, since the label is what the notebook's order reads. The list order, the name filter and the share link's `n` all read the label rather than the raw name, and the codec writes no `name` key for an unnamed entry and reads a missing one as unnamed. The row is the rename form: a control swaps the line for a field holding the stored name over a placeholder of what it falls back to, written on Enter or on losing the caret and dropped on Escape, which leaves the notebook open. Verified on a local board: an aim saved with the field empty lands as a greyed "Sans nom" whose stored entry carries no name, filing it under Chat renames the row Chat and dropping Chat falls it to Ciel, and the field writes on Enter, hands the entry back to its tag when emptied, and leaves both the name and the panel alone on Escape.

- [ ] `frontend-shell`: a bookmark stands for a square of the board and nothing else. Exit criterion: the aim offers no badge kind, the wheel sizes the only square there is, no piece badge is written, read from storage or parsed from a link, and an entry already carrying one reads as a `BADGE_PIECES_DEFAULT` square on the point it was saved at. The two kinds cost a choice before every aim and a second way to fail ("no piece here") over a spot the player can plainly see, and the tile itself says less about a place than the extract around it: a lone tab of sky names nothing, while the pile it was swept from is what the entry is about. Dropping the kind takes the toggle, the `pieceFile` half of `pickSpot`, `isPieceFile` and the `b=pieces/...` half of the share codec with it, and the four locales lose `badgeKind`, `badgeKindPiece`, `badgeKindArea` and `noPieceHere`. Nothing already saved is lost: the codec and the link parser both read a piece badge as the default square at the entry's own point, so an old notebook and an old link land on a badge that draws.

- [ ] `frontend-shell`: a new bookmark is filed under its words in the form that names it. Exit criterion: saving an aimed spot under two tags, one the notebook already holds and one new, costs no popover and no trip back to the list, the five-tag cap and the 24-character cut are enforced in the form itself, and a word written there is offered to the next entry like any other. A draft is filed through the same picker a saved row uses, opened from a button and closed again before the save: a layer over the panel and three clicks for a word the player is already typing next to it. The tags belong beside the name in the save form, a field completing on the notebook's own words that turns what is written into a chip on Enter or on a click in the suggestions, the row picker staying as it is for an entry that is already in the list.

- [ ] `frontend-shell`: the notebook reads several tags at once. Exit criterion: selecting two tags lists the bookmarks wearing both, the counter and the paging follow that list from its first page, dropping a tag widens it back, and a tag whose last bookmark loses it leaves the selection without emptying the rest of it. The view is one string today (`VIEW_ALL`, `VIEW_UNTAGGED` or `t:<tag>`), so a notebook whose words overlap can only ever be narrowed by one of them. It becomes a set of tags, intersected and not unioned, since narrowing is what a filter is for and the union of everything is what `VIEW_ALL` already answers. `tagCounts` then counts inside the current selection, so a word that would empty the list reads as zero before it is picked, and the label, the share link and the rename paths are untouched.

- [ ] `frontend-shell`: a spot is filed by a key, with the notebook closed. Exit criterion: the key files the square under the cursor at the size the aim would have used, unnamed and under the tag the list was last reading, confirms it where the player is looking, and does nothing while a field holds the caret; the entry is then found in the notebook like any other. A spot is worth keeping at the moment it matters, which is mid-sort with a hand full of pieces, and that currently costs opening the notebook, arming the aim, clicking and saving. Nothing new has to be invented for it: a name is optional and a tag is inherited, so the key is the aim run once at the cursor with the defaults it already has. Open: which key it is, and whether the confirmation is a toast or the topbar button answering.

- [ ] `frontend-canvas`: the overview's position row takes a point as well as giving one. Exit criterion: the reading turns into a field, a point written in the form it is read in (whole pieces from the centre of the frame) moves the camera there at the zoom it is already at, a point outside the play zone is clamped rather than refused, an unreadable one leaves the camera where it stands, and Escape puts the reading back. A player can read their spot out to someone, and the one they read it to has nowhere to type it, so a position only travels as a link. The row under the map is where the field belongs: it is the one control in the game that already speaks board coordinates, so reading a position and writing one are the same line and it costs no new HUD box. It keeps the row's own rules, hidden below the compact breakpoint and carried by the overview panel's Display switch.

- [x] `frontend-canvas`: the flag bar is in the order the player put it in. Exit criterion: a flag moved in the bar keeps its place across a reload, its color, its glyph on the canvas and its dot on both overviews are unaffected, and the bar still lands a dragged cluster on the flag under the pointer. The bar lists flags in the order they were dropped, which is the order the board happened to be worked in rather than the one the player thinks in, and at eight of them that order is what the bar is read by. The list already lives in `localStorage` and the color identifies a flag everywhere else, so the work is the move affordance and a codec that keeps the array order it is given, no schema and nothing on the wire. `moveFlag` already means moving a flag on the board, so the reorder is its own function. A press on a flag button is the camera jump it always was until it travels 5px, past which the button is carried under the pointer and the others slide out of its way; the slot a release would land on is read as the nearest button center in both axes, since the bar wraps inside the strip the two rails leave. The preview is `reorderFlag` itself rather than a second copy of the arithmetic, every button transformed from the slot it stands in to the one that list puts it in, which is also the number it wears while it is carried, so the key that jumps to a flag follows the drag as it happens. The order is committed once, on release, so a slot swept through costs neither a storage write nor a rollback, and Escape puts the carry back. Verified on the local 1M board: carrying the first flag to slot 3 and the last to slot 1 left the bar, the live list and `localStorage` agreeing, key 3 then jumped to the flag carried there and key 1 to the new first one, a full reload came back in the carried order, the release raised no camera jump while a 2px press still did, the canvas glyph nodes came back byte-identical (ids, colors, world and screen points), both overviews repainted pixel-identical, and every button still hit-tested to its own flag through the drop-target source the stage reads on grab.

- [ ] `frontend-shell`: how a spot is handed to someone is settled. Exit criterion: the shape is chosen, recorded in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), and built, and whatever it turns out to be, a bookmark handed over still lands the recipient framed on the same point in a browser that has never played. What exists is one URL a row, carrying the point, the badge and the name in `at`, `b` and `n`, and arriving as a draft the recipient saves under their own name. The readings left open are one link for several spots or for the whole notebook, a payload that also carries the tags and the aim size, a recipient who gets the entry saved outright or only the framing, and an affordance shorter to pass around than a URL. Whichever is taken, the three parameters stay readable: links already pasted into a chat outlive the change.

## Backlog

Ideas and open fixes worth keeping but not yet committed to a version. Promote into a version track when scope and timing are clear.

- **Locale-prefixed URLs for search.** The SEO metadata work (v1.0.0) is English-only because locale is chosen client-side with no URL segmentation, so a search engine can only index one language version of `/`. Ranking for non-English queries needs a crawlable URL per locale (`/fr/`, `/es/`, `/de/`), native-language meta strings, hreflang alternates, and rewiring every internal link to a locale-aware path: a router restructuring, not a metadata tweak. See [DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md#2026-08-17-frontend-shell-seo-metadata-is-english-only-corrected-per-route-in-js-rather-than-per-locale).
- **Dynamic max-zoom that grows with progress.** Cap zoom-out early and relax it as pieces are placed, to bound the visible piece count. A fixed 15% zoom floor already exists (see [play-zone hard limits](DECISIONS.md#2026-05-21-frontend-canvas-play-zone-hard-limits)); the progress-relative version is the open idea.
- **Peer cursor scope entry and exit announced on the wire.** A cursor is relayed only to the clients subscribed to the one world-grid cell its point falls in, and neither end of that scope is announced. Entering: a player who jumps into a busy area sees nobody until each peer happens to move their mouse. Leaving: a peer who jumps away goes silent, indistinguishable from one who let go of the mouse, so their pointer stands there until v1.1.1's 10s expiry retires it. The server can answer both from state it already holds, by keeping each connection's last cursor and the cell it fell in: answer a `viewport` entering new cells with the cursors now in scope, and on a cursor crossing cells tell the old cell's subscribers (minus the new cell's) that the pointer is gone. One extra broadcast per cell crossing rather than per message, over subscriber sets a scoped broadcast already walks. Costs two new server messages.
- **Firewall the origin to Cloudflare IP ranges.** Closes the last DDoS gap: the VPS is still directly reachable so the edge is bypassable and `CF-Connecting-IP` is spoofable. Steps in [DECISIONS topology](DECISIONS.md#2026-05-18-infra-deploy-alpha-topology).
- **Per-piece attribution.** Who placed a given piece, and when, surfaced on a locked piece in the canvas. The data model already answers it with no new storage (the first `ClusterMerge` by `at` whose `droppedPieceIds` contains the piece), so the work is a lookup route, the matching index, and the UI affordance. Pairs with the contributors explainer: it makes the scoring rule visible on the board itself.
- **Timelapse.** Replay the board's assembly by walking `ClusterMerge` in `at` order, geometry reconstructed from `generationSeed`. Designed for since the start, never built, and a project in its own right rather than a feature to slip into another chantier. The obvious payoff is shareable footage of a months-long build.
- **`GET /auth/session` answers with more than the session user.** Auth.js's database strategy hands the callback the adapter session spread over the whole user document and returns whatever the callback returns, so the response body carries the raw `sessionToken` (the credential the cookie keeps `httpOnly`) and every field on the user, `claimTokenHash` included. Trimming it to the fields the SPA reads is a few lines in the `session` callback in `auth.ts`; the exposure only pays off for an attacker who already has script execution on the app origin, which is why it is here rather than in a version.
- **Movable trays.** A container placed on the board that carries the pieces it holds when it is dragged, the digital equivalent of pushing a swept pile somewhere else. The design question is what a tray is on the wire: a purely visual frame is client-side and free, like the personal flags, but then the pieces inside it are not really in it and a peer sees a pile that does not move; a tray that actually carries its contents moves N clusters per drag, a bulk position write well outside the drag/drop/snap tiers. Sending a cluster to a named spot already exists in the flag bar drop ([DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md#2026-08-24-frontend-canvas-a-cluster-is-sent-to-a-flag-by-dropping-it-on-the-hud-bar-onto-a-searched-clear-patch)), so what is open is the physical object itself.
- **Named flags.** A flag is identified by its color and nothing else ([DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md#2026-08-24-frontend-canvas-personal-flags-are-a-per-browser-client-model-with-a-board-scaled-canvas-glyph)), which reads fine at two or three flags and stops reading at eight. A short player-written label costs no schema, since flags already live in `localStorage`; the work is an edit affordance in the bar, a length cap, and a decision on whether the label rides the canvas glyph and both overviews or stays in the HUD bar alone.
