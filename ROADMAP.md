# Roadmap

Work is tracked by version, numbered `major.minor.patch`. v1.3.0 is live in prod and v1.4.0 is open, filled as backlog items are promoted into it. The eleven tracks below cut across every version. A version ships only when its exit criterion is met, and each task carries an exit criterion, not a description. A released version is written up here as prose rather than kept as a task list; only the open version carries checkboxes. Detail on non-obvious choices lives in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

Statuses: `[ ]` not started, `[~]` in progress, `[x]` done.

## Tracks

1. `shared-protocol`: shared TS types, WS messages, piece and user schemas
2. `piece-generation`: Bezier silhouettes, snap geometry, piece types
3. `image-pipeline`: libvips processing, Deep Zoom tiles, per-piece AVIF, R2 upload
4. `frontend-shell`: landing, routing, entry flow, auth and options modals
5. `frontend-canvas`: PixiJS rendering, OpenSeadragon, drag/drop, LOD, culling
6. `backend-realtime`: WS server, drag/drop/snap logic, Redis state, Mongo logs, snapshots
7. `auth-and-accounts`: Auth.js (Google), guest players, pseudo onboarding, sessions, login anti-abuse
8. `infra-deploy`: Docker, Coolify, OVH, Cloudflare (Pages, R2, CDN, DNS)
9. `tooling-foundations`: monorepo workspaces, shared tsconfig, eslint, prettier
10. `qa-and-load`: load tests up to 1M simulated pieces and clients
11. `legal`: privacy policy, ToS, GDPR notes, license attributions

## Before v1.0.0

Four milestones, all met, condensed to what they achieved.

A single player first completed a puzzle of configurable size in a browser, with the full architecture running locally (WS server, Redis and Mongo under docker-compose) in anonymous mode. A closed alpha then put 5 to 20 invited people on a deployed 10 000-piece board at the same time, with the performance work built as the real solution rather than a stopgap: drag coalescing, per-group dispatch queues, zoom-out LOD and per-IP rate limiting. Viewport and write sharding stayed deferred, blocked on the single-writer topology rather than on piece count.

The board then opened to the public with 1 000 000 unique pieces on a single shared canvas: the gigapixel image pipeline on R2, viewport-sharded broadcasts backed by a group index and partial-state resync, PixiJS LOD with viewport-driven texture streaming, Auth.js with Google, the admin ops page, the frontend on Cloudflare Pages, EN/FR/ES/DE throughout, the legal documents, and load tests to 10 000 clients. Open access then removed the last barrier standing in front of the canvas: a first-time visitor places a piece with no OAuth redirect, on a guest identity minted in-site with the pseudo and country asked in-app, and signing in with Google claims those contributions under one persistent identity. Everyone is served by a single real-time path, gated by an admission queue past a global connection cap, and the spectator read-path was retired down to its DNS record.

## v1.0.0, Launch Readiness, RELEASED

**Exit criterion (met)**: the puzzle serves the real photo mosaic at full production scale; locked pieces render from server-composited tiles with resident memory inside budget past 995 000 locked pieces and no unlocked cluster exceeding `MPP_CLUSTER_PIECE_CAP`; player diagnostics, self-hosted analytics, and search discoverability are all live in prod; a clustered load-test run against prod passes a clean visual and state-corruption check.

The board stopped being synthetic. Three scripts (`fetch-tile-images.ts`, `ingest-unsplash-tiles.ts`, `build-mosaic.ts`) assemble a gigapixel BigTIFF photo mosaic end to end, banded chunk and strip streaming throughout, from the NASA Blue Marble main image and a combined CC0/Wikimedia and manually-downloaded Unsplash tile library, with a provenance manifest and idempotent resume. The production mosaic came out of that pipeline at 14.4 GP (full Blue Marble image, a ~10 750-photo library, a 1000x1000 grid at 120px per piece, built under Docker since Windows libvips segfaults past ~5 GP), sliced cleanly through an unchanged `slice-image.ts`, and the `earth-mosaic` assets went up to R2. Prod was cut over to that seed from the admin ops page, full wipe then switch-puzzle.

Rendering and state were rebuilt around the locked region. Locked pieces render from `DziRevealLayer`, the reference DZI pyramid revealed through a server-baked per-piece silhouette mask plus a seam overlay, decoupled from `GroupNode` and `hydrateGroup`, and `CellCompositor`'s photo tier was retired with it. Server side, a locked piece lives on the piece hash rather than in a group: `detectSnap` reads the piece-level `locked` flag and an anchored cluster's group is deleted instead of merged into one ever-growing locked group, which closed an unbounded-hydration crash risk before it was ever hit live and held at ~995 000 pieces. Unlocked-unlocked merges are capped at `MPP_CLUSTER_PIECE_CAP` (20 000), merging into a locked neighbour exempt, and `stateInvariants.ts` validates `locked` by replaying Mongo against the Redis piece flags. Locked-region state reaches clients as incremental server-composited per-cell tiles (sharp, a debounced dirty-cell queue, cached and versioned in R2, pushed live over `region_state` and `cell_composite`) instead of per-piece fetches. A board whose stored `generationSeed` differs from the one the server loads is relabeled onto the loaded seed at boot, crash-resumable and refusing anything already merged or locked, rather than being served unplayable; prod was relabeled on 2026-08-23 with its 4 618 player-moved pieces carried over, and the first lock landed minutes later.

The rest of the release made the game findable, measurable and legible. `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml` and a keyword-rich `index.html` head (canonical, Open Graph, Twitter Card, `WebApplication` JSON-LD) cover the public routes, with per-route title, description, canonical and robots correction in `src/seo.ts`, since a single static canonical would mark `/privacy` and `/legal` as duplicates of `/`. Umami and its Postgres run alongside the app under Coolify with their own DNS and TLS, the tracking script inert outside prod, Umami's own SPA route tracking separating `/play` from `/` with no extra code, and an operator self-exclusion link on the admin page. The overview header carries a live connected-player count next to its status dot, read off the existing join and leave broadcast with no new WS message. Onboarding's pseudo and country steps became independently skippable behind a Skip control, skipping the pseudo minting a unique "Guest #XXXX" and skipping the country setting the international nationality. The privacy policy discloses the self-hosted analytics and its hashing mechanism, and the Legal page credits the Unsplash sources and the mosaic-algorithm inspiration, linked from the reference image caption. Load-test bots learned to discover pieces empirically and to cluster a share of their viewports on a shared hotspot (`--cluster-fraction`), drag targets sampled from each bot's own viewport, cursors easing toward the next grab and carrying a `pseudo` name tag; the clustered run against prod was confirmed visually, labeled peer cursors gliding and dragging near each other in one viewport, with a clean PASS verdict and a clean state-corruption pass.

## v1.1.0, RELEASED

**Exit criterion (met)**: everything below shipped to prod.

A phone gets the board instead of a HUD: below the 680px breakpoint the contributors list, the live activity and the zoom controls are not rendered, leaving the reference thumbnail in one corner and the overview in the other, and all three come back on a widening viewport with no reload. The account menu gained a stepped tips strip (one-line hints between two arrows, no screen of its own), an info control on the contributors panel opening a scoring explainer (one point per piece credited once, why the standings run ahead of the locked counter, why snapping a 30-piece cluster credits one point and not thirty), and update notes: a modal listing the shipped versions newest first, each a dated heading over player-facing one-liners, seeded with v1.1.0 and the v1.0.0 launch. Its rows run sync, Discord, pseudo, country, update notes, so the two profile edits sit together and the outward links lead, and the shell scrolls past a phone viewport instead of overflowing it.

The standings became live. A scoring merge marks its contributor dirty and a coalescing publisher sends only the rows that moved inside the top 100, at most one publish per 2s window with the first merge of a quiet window going out immediately, folded client-side into the list already held; a contributor ranked outside that top 100 gets their own tally and rank on their own socket, on join and whenever it moves, so the panel pins their row instead of showing them nothing. An anchoring merge credits the whole set it locks rather than the dragged side alone, so every piece on a finished board has exactly one owner and the standings add up to the full million; the log replay unions the same two arrays, so prod's existing merges were re-credited at the next boot with no migration.

The canvas gained personal orientation tools. A desktop player keeps up to 8 personal flags on the board, created from a `+` control in a bottom-center HUD bar and persisted per browser in `localStorage` under the puzzle id; clicking a flag's button, or pressing its 1-8 key, centers the camera on it instantly and carries a sticky-carried cluster along. Each flag draws one and a half pieces tall at every zoom and on both overviews in its own color, can be dragged aside within the play zone, and opens a popover with a delete action and the 8-color palette, where picking a color already held by another flag swaps the two. A cluster is sent to a flag by dropping it on that button: it shrinks while it hovers there, edge-pan suppressed over the bar so its screen band no longer scrolls the board, and it lands on a clear patch beside the flag, searched outward against the locally known clusters and locked pieces, with the camera left exactly where the player was working. Only the HUD bar takes the drop, not the canvas or the overview markers, and a camera jump made while a cluster is carried (a flag button, a 1-8 key, the overview, fit/center) re-glues it to the cursor before the residency pass rather than after, so it is no longer dehydrated for having been left behind at the old spot. The bar is hidden under the responsive breakpoint, the toast and the carry hint moving above it. A player can also show the source photo faintly under the board, so a piece's home is visible before anything around it is locked: `DziRevealLayer` mirrors the DZI tiles it already streams into an unmasked layer above the frame and below every piece, at 0.32 group opacity, and locked content stays unmistakably on top of that ghost by casting a 3-screen-pixel shadow drawn from the combined mask. It is a player display setting, persisted per browser and reached from a Display section in the options menu.

Signing in with Google now produces a permanent account that reads as one: the sign-in promotes the guest document the provider account is linked onto (`guest` cleared, OAuth email, name and image stored) instead of leaving it flagged as a guest, and the options menu answers with a checked "Synced with Google" row carrying the linked address in place of the action, both provider entries carrying their mark (the Google G, the Discord logo); the seven accounts already linked in prod were backfilled from the identity in their stored `id_token`. A player gets back into a synced account from a second device or after the session cookie ages out through the onboarding pseudo step, the one step with no session, where Auth.js resolves the account straight to its profile; a player already minted as a guest is offered the switch instead of Auth.js's refusal page, dropping the session so the next attempt lands in the account and folding this browser's guest into it, credits included. Claim tokens survive a sign-in abandoned at Google, an absorbed guest never renames the account, and the live standings follow the fold.

A restart or an outage never reads as the player's own connection failing: the server announces the shutdown (maintenance notice, then WS close 1012) before dropping sockets, and every client, in game, arriving or on the landing, lands on a localized "puzzle unavailable, back in a few minutes" screen served entirely from Cloudflare Pages, which reloads itself back into the board once the server answers again.

## v1.2.0, RELEASED

**Exit criterion (met)**: everything below shipped to prod.

The HUD became configurable and viewport-aware. Every panel has its own switch in the Display section of the options menu (reference, zoom, activity, contributors, overview, flags), the choice kept per browser like the rest of the display preferences, and a panel nobody has touched follows its viewport default; switching one off never moves another, so on the left rail, where the zoom pillar sits between its two neighbours, a switched-off panel is hidden where it stands instead of dropped from the column. `/play` lays out inside every viewport it opens in: a compact one (at most 680px wide or 480px tall, so a phone either way up) draws the reference thumbnail and the overview alone, the contributors list, the activity panel, the zoom pillar and the flag bar neither rendered nor offered in the Display section there; above that, neither rail can overflow the stage (the two aspect-bound panels size themselves against the height left for them, the two list panels scroll their own list), the flag bar lays out in the strip the rails leave rather than under them, and every modal is bounded by the viewport it opens in and scrolls inside itself. Verified by measured rects across 13 sizes from 320x568 to 1920x1080. A release nobody has read marks itself with an accent dot on the topbar gear and on the options menu's Update notes row, both answering the newest version in `releases.ts` against the last one this browser was shown (`mpp.updatesSeen`): opening the notes clears both, opening the menu that links to them does not, a browser with nothing stored is seeded as read so a first-time player's settings menu is never marked new, and the mark reaches the accessible name of both buttons. The landing stopped announcing a board that is not finished: its percentage floors the locked/total ratio to three decimals on the integer ratio, so the last 500 pieces read 99.950% to 99.999% where they used to read 100.0% and 63 pieces read 0.006% rather than 0.01%, a finished board dropping the decimals to read 100%, the percent sign coming from Intl so fr, es and de carry their no-break space.

The overview's expand button opens the panel's own map at a readable size instead of a separate view: one painter (`canvas/overviewView.ts`) draws both, so the enlarged map shows the same density grid, piece dots, frame, frustum and flags, tracking the board frame by frame. The panel pauses its own paint while the modal is open, and a press jumps the camera under the plain arrow rather than a grab hand. Verified on the local 1M board.

Three server-side fixes closed the release. A locked piece straddling a world-grid cell boundary renders whole instead of cut off along a straight line partway across itself: `WORLD_TILE_SIZE` is not a multiple of `pieceSize`, so 55 of the 1000 columns and 55 of the 1000 rows cross a boundary, and a lock only ever marked the cell owning the piece's canonical position dirty, the owning cell's composite canvas stopping one margin past the boundary while the piece runs almost a full `pieceSize` further, leaving the rest of its silhouette in no composite at all until an unrelated later lock in the neighbouring cell happened to rebake it; a lock now marks every cell the piece's own box reaches into, 1 to 4 and never wider than the halo that would draw it. Cells baked before this shipped keep their gap until their next rebake, and no resweep is planned. A cluster dropped on a flag lands on a patch that is actually free, including in board this client has never streamed, where the local search has nothing to avoid and stacks it on pieces it cannot see: the landing spot is resolved by the server against its own complete indexes and dropped through the normal path, merge detection included, the client's own search staying the optimistic placement the authoritative echo corrects, and a flag standing in a cell already at `tilePieceCap` no longer bounces the piece back to where it was picked up. A dropped socket names its own cause instead of both ends going silent: the server logs every close with its code, its reason and how long the connection lived, and tags the two closes it issues itself (a heartbeat reap on a missed pong, a 1013 slow-consumer close with the buffered bytes that tripped it), while the client logs the close code, whether the frame was clean, and the longest stretch its own main thread went unserviced, which is what separates a background tab the browser stopped servicing from a network fault and from a server-side reap.

## v1.3.0, RELEASED

**Exit criterion (met)**: everything below shipped to prod.

The game stopped speaking as a competition. No user-facing string in the four locales calls the standings a leaderboard, a ranking, a score or a point: the panel, its modal, the counting explainer, the nationality lede, the update notes and the privacy page all speak of contributors and of pieces counted once, while the rank column and its top-three emphasis are left as they are. Every window on `/play` also gained one key, carried across component filenames, root CSS class, i18n namespace and HUD panel id, the overview no longer called a minimap anywhere a player can read it; the wire keeps its `minimap` and `leaderboard` names, so that half needed no backend deploy. A nationality flag names its country in the reader's language wherever it appears (the contributors people and country lists, the topbar presence, the nationality picker's preview), and the picker is both read and ordered on the localized name.

A map you can read became a map you can aim from. A press on the enlarged overview jumps the camera exactly as the panel does, sweeping with the pointer and leaving the modal open so the frustum tracks it, both views inverting one shared mapping; a quick click on the enlarged reference centers the board on the world point under it, armed either by a crosshair toggle in the control pillar (labelled in the four locales) or by Ctrl/Cmd, with an unarmed plain click still zooming the photo as before. That enlarged reference also keeps its border at every zoom: a pan hard against any image edge stops with the same empty band the fitted view rests in (5% of the viewer on that axis), the fitted view is the zoom floor and cannot be dragged off center, and the band re-derives from the viewer when the modal is resized. The board's own edge now stops the camera where it becomes visible, the padding ring being a fraction of the viewport rather than of the play zone, where the 1M board's zone let it travel some 27 000 world units (16 screens at zoom 1) past that border, and the out-of-bounds checker covers every point the clamp can reach so no pan ends on the bare stage backdrop. The zoom-out LOD band is really overview-only: no press below `LOD_ENTER_ZOOM` starts a grab or a sticky carry, including on a cluster the band keeps drawn live (held by a peer, or in a cell not yet baked) and on a hidden cluster reached by the strict hit-test's fall-through, such a press panning instead and the pointer over those clusters no longer reading as a grab hand, while a press-drag or a carry begun above the band still commits its drop inside it.

The landing stopped needing a reload, and stopped rendering a phase it has not confirmed. An open page picks up a new placement, the locked count and the contributors list one poll after the server sees them, reading a global `GET /live` an edge can cache rather than the per-IP `GET /landing`; a failed poll leaves the page exactly as it is instead of raising the maintenance screen, and no poll runs while the board is not live or the tab is hidden. A reload shows neither the pre-launch countdown nor its "interested" call to action before `GET /landing` has answered or failed, and the space they occupy holds its height so the hero does not jump when the answer lands. The route reaches the edge cache through a Cloudflare Cache Rule marking the path eligible, JSON not being cached on response headers alone; that rule also rewrites the origin's `max-age=0` on its way out, which would let a browser answer its own poll for hours, so the poll asks for `cache: "no-store"` and leaves the edge to absorb the load through `s-maxage` alone.

Two server-side rules closed the board's remaining soft spots. Stacking pieces on one spot no longer brute-forces snaps: a loose-loose merge is refused when more than `MPP_SNAP_COVER_MAX` (5) other loose pieces stand on the contact piece's centre, the drop still resting where it landed and its author alone getting a toast saying why in the four locales, a ragged cluster's own bounding-box gaps never counting against it, and the anchoring path exempt so nothing dumped on the frontier can block the board's progression. And a drop cannot park a cluster off the board: `handleDrop` clamps the origin it persists into the play zone with the same body-aware formula the client and the flag landing already use, so a forged drop (the wire only proves its coordinates are finite) rests at the zone edge instead of anywhere unreachable in the coordinate space, every honest drop, which the client already clamps more tightly, untouched.

The release also opened the host's own backups up and cleared three smaller obstructions. Every upload had been failing outright, since a bucket-scoped R2 token holds no bucket-level rights and rclone's pre-upload existence check is refused, so the pass skips that check and its R2 write path works for the first time: the Mongo dump reaches `mpp-backups`. The sidecar also reaches wider, joining Umami's and Coolify's own Docker networks and mounting `/data/coolify` read-only for the `APP_KEY` without which the Coolify database dump decrypts nothing. Carrying a pass past that Mongo dump is still open, in the backlog below. Both OpenSeadragon viewers request their DZI tiles with CORS, so a tile the reference fetched first stays loadable by the board's own `Assets.load`, and a deep zoom in the enlarged view followed by panning the board onto that same area draws the tiles of the current level instead of falling back to the base one. Under 560px the topbar carries one thing per box, the mark, the full `locked / total` ratio and the pseudo before the gear, having dropped the wordmark, the progress bar and the presence flag that crowded them out, a pseudo too long for its column ending in an ellipsis and nothing overflowing the row from 320px up, the bar untouched above 560px. And the contributors modal's people/countries switch hugs its two buttons again instead of stretching to the shell width.

## v1.4.0

**Exit criterion**: everything below is deployed and verified in prod.

- [~] `frontend-shell`: the landing's live activity fills the card it sits in. Exit criterion: on the live landing, the activity feed and the contributors list end level, with no band of empty card under the last placement. The two cards sit side by side and stretch to the taller one, where a contributor row measures 36px against an activity line's 16px, so six entries each left 90px empty under the feed; the feed's depth is now its own constant (`LIVE_ACTIVITY_LIMIT`, 10) rather than the contributors' (`LIVE_CONTRIBUTORS_LIMIT`, 6), applied to both routes the landing reads so its first paint and its first poll agree, while the in-game ticker's own backfill stays at the six its panel holds. Needs the backend redeploy before it can be verified.
- [~] `frontend-canvas`: a viewport arriving on a dense region fills outward from its centre, leaving no hole where the player is looking. Exit criterion: on an arrival dense enough to saturate the hydrate queue, every texture load resolves inside its deadline and the median time to a group's first load rises from the centre of the screen to its corners. Two things decided what a player saw. The hydrate queue ranked on squared distance to the viewport centre alone, which ranks against a point while the viewport is a rectangle, so a group in the hydrate margin (`HYDRATE_MARGIN_FRAC`, 0.3 of the viewport beyond it) near a centre axis outranked one in the viewport's own corner; the queue is now partitioned, on-screen groups ahead of margin-ring ones, distance kept as the tie-break inside each block, which on a 404-group screen served it whole by rank 404 instead of rank 723. And every texture went straight to `Assets.load`, whose image-decode pool (`navigator.hardwareConcurrency` workers) dispatches its own backlog last-in-first-out: at 128 concurrent loads the ones that missed a worker sat at the bottom of that stack while the pump kept pushing newer loads on top, so the groups submitted first, the nearest ones and so the middle of the screen, resolved only on `TEXTURE_LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS` (10s) plus a retry that re-awaited the same pending promise for 10s more. Measured on the local 1M board: 102 of an arrival's 1805 loads failed at 20 027 ms, the pump ran on 26 of its 128 slots from t=10s to t=20s, and the centre of the screen stayed blank that whole time. Loads are now admitted one per pool worker, so the pool never holds a backlog: on the same arrival, no load over 9s (slowest 1696 ms), ~90 loads a second sustained, and time to first load running 0.9s at the centre to 4.9s at the corners. Needs the frontend deploy before it can be confirmed on the live board.

## Backlog

Ideas and open fixes worth keeping but not yet committed to a version. Promote into a version track when scope and timing are clear.

- **Locale-prefixed URLs for search.** The SEO metadata work (v1.0.0) is English-only because locale is chosen client-side (`localStorage`/browser language) with no URL segmentation, so a search engine can only index one language version of `/`. Ranking for non-English queries (e.g. French "puzzle le plus grand") needs its own crawlable URL per locale (`/fr/`, `/es/`, `/de/`), native-language meta strings, hreflang alternates, and rewiring every internal link (language switcher, router pushes) to a locale-aware path: a router restructuring, not a metadata tweak. See [DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md#2026-08-17-frontend-shell-seo-metadata-is-english-only-corrected-per-route-in-js-rather-than-per-locale).
- **Dynamic max-zoom that grows with progress.** Cap zoom-out early and relax it as pieces are placed, to bound the visible piece count. A fixed 15% zoom floor already exists (see [play-zone hard limits](DECISIONS.md#2026-05-21-frontend-canvas-play-zone-hard-limits)); the progress-relative version is the open idea.
- **Peer cursor scope entry and exit announced on the wire.** A cursor is relayed only to the clients subscribed to the one world-grid cell its point falls in, and neither end of that scope is announced, so both transitions are guesswork on the client. Entering: a player who jumps into a busy area sees nobody there until each peer happens to move their mouse. Leaving: a peer who jumps away simply goes silent, indistinguishable from one who let go of the mouse, so their pointer stands where they left it until v1.1.1's client-side expiry retires it 10s later. The server can answer both from state it already holds, by keeping each connection's last cursor and the cell it fell in: answer a `viewport` that enters new cells with the cursors of the peers now in scope, and on a cursor crossing into another cell tell the old cell's subscribers (minus the new cell's) that the pointer is gone. That is one extra broadcast per cell crossing rather than per message, walking the same cell subscriber sets a scoped broadcast already walks. Costs two new server messages.
- **Firewall the origin to Cloudflare IP ranges.** Closes the last DDoS gap: the VPS is still directly reachable so the edge is bypassable and `CF-Connecting-IP` is spoofable. Steps in [DECISIONS topology](DECISIONS.md#2026-05-18-infra-deploy-alpha-topology).
- **Shareable viewport deep link.** `/play` accepting a position and zoom in the query string, so a spot on the board can be linked in Discord ("help here") or bookmarked outside the app. Sibling of the personal flags and the natural way to share one, which they deliberately are not (strictly personal, client-side). No secret is exposed: board coordinates are already client-visible, unlike the seed-permuted piece ids.
- **Per-piece attribution.** Who placed a given piece, and when, surfaced on a locked piece in the canvas. The data model already answers it with no new storage (the first `ClusterMerge` by `at` whose `droppedPieceIds` contains the piece), so the work is a lookup route, the matching index, and the UI affordance. Pairs with the contributors explainer: it makes the scoring rule visible on the board itself.
- **Timelapse.** Replay the board's assembly by walking `ClusterMerge` in `at` order, geometry reconstructed from `generationSeed`. Designed for since the start, never built, and a project in its own right rather than a feature to slip into another chantier. The obvious payoff is shareable footage of a months-long build.
- **`GET /auth/session` answers with more than the session user.** Auth.js's database strategy hands the callback the adapter session spread over the whole user document and returns whatever the callback returns, so the response body carries the raw `sessionToken` (the credential the cookie keeps `httpOnly`) and every field on the user, `claimTokenHash` included. Trimming it to the fields the SPA reads is a few lines in the `session` callback in `auth.ts`; the exposure only pays off for an attacker who already has script execution on the app origin, which is why it is here rather than in a version.
- **Movable trays.** A container placed on the board that carries the pieces it holds when it is dragged, the digital equivalent of sweeping a handful of pieces to the side of a real table and pushing the whole pile somewhere else. The design question is what a tray is on the wire: a purely visual frame is client-side and free, like the personal flags, but then the pieces inside it are not really in it and a peer sees a pile that does not move; a tray that actually carries its contents moves N clusters per drag, which is a bulk position write and a broadcast well outside the drag/drop/snap tiers the event hierarchy is built on. Sending a cluster to a named spot already exists in the flag bar drop ([DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md#2026-08-24-frontend-canvas-a-cluster-is-sent-to-a-flag-by-dropping-it-on-the-hud-bar-onto-a-searched-clear-patch)), so the cheap half of the idea is already shipped and what is open is the physical object itself.
- **Named flags.** A flag is identified by its color and nothing else ([DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md#2026-08-24-frontend-canvas-personal-flags-are-a-per-browser-client-model-with-a-board-scaled-canvas-glyph)), which reads fine at two or three flags and stops reading at eight. A short player-written label per flag costs no schema, since flags already live in `localStorage`; the work is an edit affordance in the bar, a length cap, and a decision on whether the label rides the canvas glyph and both overviews or stays in the HUD bar alone, where it does not fight the board for space.
- **Ctrl-click to select multiple pieces with a maximum amount like 5 or 10 to avoid too much trolling, might need to set the held property right away to avoid conflicts.**
